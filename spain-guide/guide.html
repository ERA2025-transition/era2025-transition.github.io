The Spain Road Trip Starter

There is one great Spain road trip, and then there are three ways to do a piece of it.

🔴 THE FULL RUN

Galicia → Asturias → Basque Country → Castilla → Andalucía → Costa del Sol

North to south, coast to coast. About 3,000 km over 30 days. This is the one I am driving right now, and it is the only route where you watch the whole country change underneath you. Green cliffs and cider country at the top. Roman stone and castles through the middle. Moorish palaces and warm water at the bottom.

Nobody tells you Spain is six countries wearing one flag. You find that out somewhere around day twelve, when the rain stops and the light goes gold.

Pick this if: you have three weeks or more and you want the trip you will still be talking about in ten years.

Skip it if: you have under two weeks. Take one leg properly instead of all three badly.

Short on time? Take a leg. Each one stands alone.

1. Leg One, the Green North

Santiago de Compostela → Cíes Islands → Ribeira Sacra → Covadonga → Cudillero → Santillana del Mar → Gaztelugatxe → Bilbao → San Sebastián → Haro

10 days, roughly 900 km. The Spain nobody posts. Atlantic cliffs, fishing villages built into ravines, a hermitage on a sea stack you climb 241 steps to reach. And the best food in the country, full stop.

Pick this if: you eat your way through countries, you hate crowds, or you are going in July and August when the south is an oven.

Skip it if: you need guaranteed sun. It rains. That is the deal, and it is worth it.

2. Leg Two, the Castilian Middle

Segovia → Ávila → Madrid → Toledo → Consuegra → Cáceres → Mérida

8 days, roughly 700 km. A Roman aqueduct holding itself up with no mortar. City walls you can walk the whole way around. Windmills on a ridge. A Roman theatre still standing where they still put on plays.

The cheapest leg by a wide margin, and the least touristed.

Pick this if: you love history, you are watching the budget, or you want a shoulder-season trip when the coast is dead.

Skip it if: you need a beach at the end of it.

3. Leg Three, the Golden South

Córdoba → Seville → Cádiz → Vejer → Ronda → Caminito del Rey → Marbella → Nerja → Granada

12 days, roughly 900 km. The one everybody means when they say Spain. The Mezquita's striped arches. Flamenco in a Seville courtyard. A bridge over a gorge at Ronda. The Alhambra at golden hour, which is worth the entire trip on its own.

Pick this if: it is your first Spain and you want the photos you have been saving.

Skip it if: you are going in August. Córdoba hits 45°C and no photo is worth that.

Every stop above is on the feed. I am posting one a day for 30 days, so you can see each place before you commit to it. Follow along and steal whichever ones you want.

🔴 THE HACK

Here is the thing that ruins Spain road trips, and almost nobody warns you.

Every Spanish city over 50,000 people now has a low emission zone. They're called ZBE, Zonas de Bajas Emisiones. Drive into one without the right clearance and the fine is €200. Your rental company then adds €30 to €50 in admin fees for the privilege of passing it on to you. It's per entry. There is no tourist exemption.

Now the good news, and this is the whole hack:

Your rental car is almost certainly fine. Rental fleets in Spain average under three years old, so practically anything you pick up at Málaga, Barcelona, or Madrid airport already carries at least a "C" environmental sticker, which gets you into every zone in the country. Ask the agent to point at the windscreen sticker when you collect the car. Ten seconds. Done.

The trap is only if you drive your own car in. Foreign plates can't get a Spanish DGT sticker. Barcelona makes you pre-register at zberegistre.ambmobilitat.cat before you enter, it costs €7, and verification takes up to 15 working days. Miss that and you're paying fines you won't hear about until you're home.

And the part that actually saves your trip: don't drive into historic centres at all. Not because of the zones. Because the streets were built for donkeys, your GPS will confidently send you down one that narrows to nothing, and you will reverse out of it in front of an audience. Park in a signed underground car park on the edge of the old town, €12 to €20 a day, and walk in. Every single time.

Two more numbers worth knowing:

The AP-7 along the entire Mediterranean coast has been free since September 2021, as has the AP-2. The Madrid radials are free too. So the classic coastal drive costs you nothing but fuel.

Tolls that remain: AP-68 Bilbao to Zaragoza (~€33), AP-9 through Galicia (~€22), AP-6 out of Madrid (~€10), AP-66 to Asturias (~€12). Card works at every booth. If you're doing Green Spain, budget about €70 in tolls for the whole route and stop thinking about it.

The Permission Slip

Nobody is going to hand you this trip. Here's how you take it.

1. Pick the route above. Right now, before you close this.

Not the best one. The one that sounded like you. You already know which one it was.

2. Open a calendar and put two dates on it.

Departure and return. Not "spring." Not "when work calms down." Two actual squares on an actual calendar. This is the step everybody skips and it's the only one that matters.

3. Price the flight today. Don't book it yet.

You need the real number, not the imagined one. It's almost always lower than the story you've been telling yourself.

4. Book the car before the hotels.

This is backwards from what you'd think and it's correct. Automatics sell out in Spain and cost more, most of the fleet is manual, and one-way drop-offs need locking in early. The car decides the shape of the trip.

5. Tell one person.

Out loud. The friend who'd fly across the world to match your red. Say the dates. Once someone else knows, you're going.

That's it. That's the whole permission slip. You signed it yourself.
